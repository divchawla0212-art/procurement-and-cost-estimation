What it costs to run, at five packages a month

Five tender packages a month, 80 MB of documents each — 400 MB a month. This is the cost to operate: the AI bill and the server bill. It excludes anything spent building the system.

The $120 planning figure is that $110 with a little room for document revisions. Everything below explains where each piece comes from and what moves it.

The method, in one line

You do not need the derivation to use this. The whole model collapses to a rate per megabyte, a waste multiplier, and a flat server bill.

monthly cost = (MB per month × rate) × waste + server
= (400 × $0.065) × 1.5 + $80
= $26 × 1.5 + $80 = $119

Rate is about 6.5 cents per megabyte on Sonnet 5 (11 cents on Opus 5, 2 cents on Haiku 4.5). Waste covers retries and re-reading revised documents — 1.5 is a sensible default. Server is flat at this scale.

Rule of thumb

Seven cents a megabyte, plus eighty dollars a month. That is close enough for budgeting at any volume up to about 40 GB a month, which is roughly a hundred times what you are doing now.

Where the AI number comes from

Four steps take megabytes of PDF to dollars. Each constant was measured by replaying a real tender through the actual code, not estimated — the two exceptions are flagged.

Step What happens For 400 MB Confidence
1 PDF becomes text. A megabyte of PDF is mostly not text. Measured at 18,470 usable characters per MB. 7.4M characters Corpus-dependent
2 Text becomes what we actually send. Documents are split into chunks and each chunk re-sends its instructions, so ~1.46× the text goes over the wire. 10.8M characters Measured
3 Characters become tokens — the unit Anthropic bills in. Calibrated at 3.41 characters per token in, 2.06 out. 4.27M tokens Measured
4 Tokens become dollars at the published list price. $25.98 List price

The split is 74% reading, 26% writing — 3.17M tokens in, 1.10M out. That matters because writing costs five times more per token than reading, so the smaller half of the traffic is the bigger half of the bill.

What makes it go up

Three things add cost on top of the clean number. The first two are what you asked about.

1. Failed calls and reruns  +15% typical

When a document chunk is too dense, the model hits its output ceiling and the call fails. The system retries once. Three details make this worse than a simple retry rate suggests:

  • The failure is deterministic. If a chunk is too big, it is too big on the retry as well — so you pay for two failed calls, not one.
  • Merging is all-or-nothing. One chunk failing twice discards the whole document's work — after every one of its chunks has already been billed. A 220,000-character proposal is about 19 chunks, so 19 chances to lose all 19.
  • Failures are not cached. Successful documents are skipped on the next run; failed ones are retried at full price every single run.
The one real leak

A document that reliably fails costs money every week, forever, and produces nothing. It is not a rounding error — it is a permanent subscription to a failure. The fix is configuration, not spend: raise the output ceiling or lower the chunk size. Switching to a more expensive model does not fix it — same ceiling.

2. Revised vendor documents  ×1.3 – ×2.0

A vendor issuing Rev. B of a proposal means that document is read again from scratch. There is no partial re-read and no diffing — a revision costs the same as the original. Superseding the old revision's data is free, but that is bookkeeping, not AI work.

The striking number

Processing one package takes about 34 minutes. Five packages is 2.8 hours of work a month — out of roughly 730 hours in the month.

The server is 99.6% idle and you pay for all of it. You are not buying computing power; you are buying an address that is always answering. Nothing about this improves by processing fewer documents — it is already as low as it goes.

This is also why volume barely moves your bill. One server handles roughly 40 GB a month before capacity is a concern. You could grow a hundredfold before the server line changes at all — only the AI line would rise, at seven cents a megabyte.

How to make it cheaper

LeverSavesWhat it costs you
Batch processing — submit work asynchronously $19/mo Results arrive later, not instantly. Ingestion already runs in the background, so this is close to free.
Reserve the server for a year $21/mo A 12-month commitment. Roughly 35% off the compute line.
Fix the failing-chunk leak $5/mo Nothing — it is a configuration change. Small in money, but it stops paying indefinitely for documents that never process.
Run Haiku instead of Sonnet $26/mo Lower extraction quality. Not recommended — you would be trading accuracy for the price of a couple of coffees.

The first two are free money. Batch processing plus a reserved server takes the realistic monthly figure from $119 down to about $78 — a third off, with no loss of quality and no change to what the system does.

How much to trust these numbers

Being clear about this matters more than the precision of any single figure.

ClaimConfidenceWhy
Tokens → dollars High Published list prices, verified 24 June 2026, times arithmetic. The only risk is Anthropic changing prices.
Text → tokens High Calibrated with Anthropic's own token counter against text reproducing this corpus's exact layout.
Documents → text Medium Measured on one corpus of born-digital PDFs. A client whose documents are scanned, or spreadsheet-heavy, moves this a long way. This is the weakest link in the chain.
Failure overhead (+15%) Estimated A judgement, not a measurement. Could plausibly be 5% or 30%.
Server bill ($80–175) Estimated Priced from a standard configuration, not from an actual invoice.
Read this before quoting anyone

Nothing here has been measured from a real bill. The system has no token accounting anywhere in it — it does not currently record what any run consumed. These figures are a model built from a replayed tender, and they are the best available, but they are a projection.

Adding usage metering is the single highest-value next step if these numbers are going to support a commercial decision. Until then, treat the ±$85–290 range as real and the $120 as a planning figure.
